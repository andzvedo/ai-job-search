Bitso Business Onboarding and Registration [ 🟢 LIVE PRODUCT]

Live Product: https://bitso.com/business/register

Since it is a business registration process, I recorded this video to share part of the flow of the live product.

The idea of the Business Registration was to speed up the whole onboarding process for the business users, which was done manually internally by Bitso employees and took a long time to complete.


Bitso is a regulated cryptocurrency exchange company, and for a business to have an account approved to starts operate, it must comply with “Gibraltar + Country of operations Regulations,” providing a lot of documents about the Company, and also about the UBOs, and main Shareholders. It's very bureaucratic, and the list of documents required is long.

The main challenge was dealing with friction, and trying to reduce as much complexity as possible for the user's side, and at the same time following the regulators’ needs.

Collaboration was essential to making this work. I did a lot of back and forth with business stakeholders like Compliance officers, Legal, and KYC from the countries Bitso operates at that time (Mexico, Brazil, Argentina, and Gibraltar-Global) to understand the needs behind the regulator's request, and then together with my team, design possible solutions.

The main lessons I learned working on this product were how to do effective stakeholder management, and how Design plays a key role in not only generating alignment or ideas but also in challenging everyone to be more flexible in how to solve problems in the web3 environment, which bring a lot of new opportunities and challenges.

I hope that makes sense 😅

And to give an example of a product I designed, I'd like to share about one of the projects I'm most proud of, which happened during my time at Magazine Luiza/Luizalabs.

I worked there as a UX/UI Designer to design from scratch the MaaS platform, composed of a “Console” and an “Account.” The idea of the MaaS platform was allow Magazine Luiza and other retail stores to sell digital or physical services online (free services too) to small retailers integrated on their own e-commerce, paying based on the demand used, like Firebase does for cloud services.

I worked together with an entire cross-functional team formed by a Product Owner, Tech Lead, two Back-end Engineers, two Front-end Engineers, and one QA for more than six months.

After months of hard work, the leadership halted the entire initiative because they were unsure whether the business proposition was valuable to small retailers.

I'm proud of this project because it was the project in that I had the most learnings in my career, which I could understand that the role of a Designer goes beyond the craft.

The lessons I learned were:

  • Never work in isolation. If your team is working in a silo, something it's wrong, and you need to help change that.
  • Challenging assumptions using Design. Assumptions are a great way to help the team and business move forward, but making decisions just based on it, it's an enormous risk that can be avoided with the help of Design methods.
  • Design to have a feedback loop. Gathering feedback from people often - Stakeholders, peers, and users - are key to designing something valuable.

About design and skills

What do you think are the biggest challenges facing product designers today?

Communication for me is the biggest challenge today. Because Product Designers should speak various "languages", like Business language, or Engineers language, and knows all the nuances to express your design decisions for different audiences inside the team and the company

What do you think are the key skills necessary for success as a product designer?

Definitely is not only focused on the Design craft anymore. For me a Designer should also know about negotiation, empathy, and leadership to be able to have the possibility to create relationships and execute the best Design work possible

What do you think sets product design apart from other design disciplines?

For me Product Design is a way to apply end-to-end Interaction Design focusing on a slice of a software, and not the software as a whole, and that is a crucial difference because this impact pretty much everything in the way should be designed for a product.
