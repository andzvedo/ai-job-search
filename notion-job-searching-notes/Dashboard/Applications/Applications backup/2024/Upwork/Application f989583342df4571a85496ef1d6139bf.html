In 2019, I had the privilege of collaborating with Novidá, a Brazilian startup at the forefront of operational team supervision through innovative technology. As a freelance Product Designer, I contributed to the development of Novidá's cutting-edge platform designed to enhance managerial oversight. This platform enables real-time tracking of both employees and internal equipment's micro-locations within the workplace, facilitating a more efficient allocation of operational teams' daily activities.

Novidá's web application stands out by offering managers a dual-view of staff productivity: it not only captures tasks manually entered by users but also integrates tasks executed automatically within the system. This comprehensive approach to monitoring work processes allows for a more accurate and dynamic assessment of team performance, ultimately driving improvements in operational efficiency.

In my role, I was instrumental in transforming Novidá's responsive web application, focusing on enhancing its productivity management capabilities. The core of the redesign was to empower managers with a sophisticated tool that leverages both manually entered tasks and those executed automatically through the platform, thanks to Novidá's proprietary AI technology. This integration aimed to streamline the operational oversight and decision-making process, making it more efficient and effective.

The innovation extended to employing IoT technology for real-time data collection on employee activities, tracking the duration of each task based on the employee's physical location within the work environment. This data, combined with manual inputs from employees, was processed through our proprietary AI to present a comprehensive view on the web application.

This enhanced system enabled managers to gain a clear understanding of staff productivity. They could easily identify tasks that were completed on time, those that were delayed, and thereby make well-informed decisions. This holistic approach to monitoring and managing productivity was a game-changer, providing managers with actionable insights to optimize team performance and streamline workflows.

Bitso Card Project Overview - Leading a Cryptocurrency Card Initiative

During my time at Bitso (bitso.com) - the biggest cryptocurrency exchange Latin America, I had the opportunity to lead the design of the Bitso Card initiative.

To give a bit more context, at the end of 2021 Bitso visualized an opportunity of giving Mexican customers the possibility to pay using their crypto for the first time in the country. So Bitso created a new team: Bitso Card - Which I was invited to join.

The first step of the team was to create an MVP version and to do that, I [as Staff Product Designer], the Product Manager, the UX Researcher, and the Content Designer started a Discovery phase to investigate the current users and target audience needs, pains, and opportunities, to guide the product strategy of what should be build from the Users perspective for the MVP.

We ran a study with Bitso users that were considered the target profile we believed would be interested in having a Bitso Card. It was decided to use a mixed two methods in this study: In-depth interviews + moderated Concept testing, with the intent of gathering Attitudinal and Behavioral qualitative data.

This study brought high-level learnings/information that helped inform not only the Design decisions but also invalidate one of the most important decisions made by the business until that moment., which was to give an amount of "Points” per purchase as a benefit of using the card, but we learned that this proposal wasn't attractive enough, and instead of following that, giving Bitcoin, or another crypto as cashback, was something perceived as a strong differentiator for the users.

Those findings helped the business change its decision to give crypto instead of points, called "Cryptoback”.

In this project, I prioritized collaboration and included regular feedback sessions with the design team to gather their perspective and feedback. In those sessions, my main focus was to give enough context about the design progress, enabling the participants to share constructive feedback and leave aside personal opinions and guesses. Those meetings took place every two weeks, and everyone on the design team could join, but a couple of folks were more important because they could be working on initiatives related at the moment.
