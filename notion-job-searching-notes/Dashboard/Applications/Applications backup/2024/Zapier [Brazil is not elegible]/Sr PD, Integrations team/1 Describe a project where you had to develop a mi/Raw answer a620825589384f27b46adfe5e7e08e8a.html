Design Challenge

The Maas Console has two views, one is Public and the other is Registered.

At the Public view, the main challenge was to show the services available to hire and authenticate users integrating with the MaaS Account application because without authentication it wasn't possible to hire any service, just browse the relevant information, like details about the service, images, technical documentation of how to integrate, and plans offered.

At the Registered view, and with an organization associated, the main challenge was to transform a complex and extensive service registration process into a clear and descriptive experience for the users and organizations wanting to sell their services in the MaaS Console and hire services from others as well.

Outcome

  • The team trusted in me more than in the Product owner - Influence
  • Help to share understanding, team building, and trust

I'm proud of this project because it was the project in which I had the most learnings in my career, and I could understand that the role of a Designer goes beyond the craft.

The lessons I learned were:

  • Never work in isolation. If your team is working in a silo, something it's wrong, and you need to help change that.
  • Challenging assumptions using Design. Assumptions are a great way to help the team and business move forward, but making decisions just based on it, it's an enormous risk that can be avoided with the help of Design methods.
  • Design to have a feedback loop. Gathering feedback from people often - Stakeholders, peers, and users - are key to designing something valuable.

MaaS Ecosystem

The MaaS ecosystem, or Magalu as a Service, was the result of Magazine Luiza's shift from a retail to a tech company. Magazine Luiza is one of the largest retail companies in Brazil. The MaaS ecosystem was a developer-focused platform consisting of two applications: the MaaS Console and the Maas Account.

The MaaS Console functioned as an online marketplace for retail services. It allowed Magazine Luiza and other retail stores to sell both digital and physical services to small retailers who integrated these services into their own e-commerce platforms. This was modeled after the way Firebase provides backend services, allowing users to pay based on demand.

During the development of this project, I faced several challenges. I was the only team member without a technical background in a team composed of a Tech Lead, Product Owner, four front-end developers, two back-end developers, and a QA engineer. I had to quickly understand technical concepts such as User Management, Connections and APIs, and Endpoints.

We faced difficulties due to lack of communication and collaboration between the Product Owner and Tech Lead. The Product Owner was not always present and provided high-level epics for the team to build, without giving enough business context or clear objectives. The Tech Lead was tasked with breaking down these epics into tasks for the team. This misalignment often caused stress and frustration within the team.

I took the initiative to bridge this gap using my design work. I worked closely with the Product Owner to translate business objectives into user-centered requirements and a clear vision for the team. I also partnered with the Tech Lead to break down tasks into tangible, user-friendly tasks. Through this process, I built a strong bond with the engineers and involved them in the design process.

I'm proud of this project as it was a significant learning experience in my career. It taught me that the role of a Designer goes beyond the craft - it involves fostering team building, trust, and understanding. The lessons I learned were to never work in isolation, challenge assumptions using Design, and design to have a feedback loop.
