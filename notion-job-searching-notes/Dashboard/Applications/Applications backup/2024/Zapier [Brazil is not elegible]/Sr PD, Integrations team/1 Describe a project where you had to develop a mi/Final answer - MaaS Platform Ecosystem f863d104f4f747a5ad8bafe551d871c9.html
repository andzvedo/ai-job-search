Final answer - MaaS Platform/Ecosystem

For the project in which I developed a mid-to-long-term vision for a complex tool, I was involved in creating the MaaS (Magalu as a Service) ecosystem for Magazine Luiza. This platform, that included the MaaS Console and the MaaS Account, was a developer-focused solution that transitioned Magazine Luiza from a retail to a tech company.

The MaaS Console, modeled after Firebase's backend services, served as an online marketplace for retail services. It enabled Magazine Luiza and other retailers to offer digital and physical services to small retailers for integration into their e-commerce platforms.

As the sole team member with a non-technical background, I had to quickly familiarize myself with technical concepts such as User Management, Connections and APIs, and Endpoints.

The team, which included a Tech Lead, Product Owner, four front-end developers, two back-end developers, and a QA engineer, faced communication and collaboration challenges.

These issues were primarily due to the Product Owner's absence and the provision of high-level epics without sufficient business context or clear objectives.

To counter this lack of long-term vision, I leveraged my design skills to bridge the gap. I collaborated closely with the Product Owner, bringing him closer to the team and translating high-level business goals into a compelling product vision and actionable design steps.

Simultaneously, partnering with the engineering team was crucial for our iterative delivery. My collaboration was hands-on; I involved engineers early in the design process, seeking their input to ensure feasibility and alignment with technical constraints. Together, we adopted an agile approach, breaking down the project into small tasks, which allowed us to design, test, and refine features incrementally.

This method fostered a rhythm of continuous improvement and adaptation, which became particularly valuable when addressing unexpected issues along the road, like when we forgot to include a key feature. But instead of pointing fingers, I took some responsibility and we focused on fixing it together. This made the team trust each other more and work better together.

This project was a significant milestone in my career, teaching me the broader role of a Designer beyond crafting - it involves promoting team building, trust, and understanding. It reinforced the importance of not working in isolation, challenging assumptions through Design, and designing for a feedback loop.
