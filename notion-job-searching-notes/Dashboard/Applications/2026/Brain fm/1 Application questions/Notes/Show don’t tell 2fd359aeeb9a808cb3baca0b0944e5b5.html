I ran into an application anti-pattern the other day.

A number of people in my network are raving about

Brain.fm

, so I thought I would try it. I abandoned the free trial signup once it asked for my credit card.

"Number of users who forgot to cancel the trial" is a very poor metric indeed.

I sent them this email (in part):

"I thought I would try Brain FM on the recommendation of an acquaintance who raved about it. I don't sign up for free trials that require a credit card as this is a UX anti-pattern. Then emailing me and letting me have 3 free days, instead of 14, because I abandoned the signup without entering the credit card, with an email each day, another anti-pattern.

There was a high chance I would have kept using the product if I'd been able to try it out with no obligation and no risk of getting charged if I forgot. [Redacted personal situation details], but I don't have the mind-space to necessarily keep track of when 14 days runs out.

Please consider ditching the credit card for the free trial period, and make it something decent like 30 days instead."

This is a company that wants to help people focus, but uses a known anti-pattern that exploits people's lack of focus. Grrrrr.
