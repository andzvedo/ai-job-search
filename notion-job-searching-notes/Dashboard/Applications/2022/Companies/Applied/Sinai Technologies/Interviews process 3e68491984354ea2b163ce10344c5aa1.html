2. Tell about a complex challenge

Please describe a complex design challenge you have overcome. How did you balance user needs with other competing priorities? What was challenging? What did you learn?

Bitso Card

During my time at Bitso [The biggest cryptocurrency exchange in LatAm] I had the chance to work with two products, and one of them was Bitso Card.

The main lessons I had there were the importance of challenge business assumptions with evidences, and to be prepare to quick adapt based on new learnings.

To give a bit more of context at the end of 2021, Bitso visualized an opportunity of giving to the Mexican customers the opportunity to pay using their crypto for the first time in the country. So Bitso create a new team: Bitso Card - Which I was invited to join.

The first step of the team was create an MVP version, and to do that, Me [as Staff Product Designer], the Product Manager, the Researcher, and the Content Designer started a Discovery phase to investigate the current users and target audience real needs, pains, and opportunities, to guide the product strategy of what should be build from the Users perspective for the MVP.

We ran a study with Bitso users that were considered the target profile that we believed would be interested in to have a Bitso Card.

It was decided to use a mixed two methods in this study: In-depth interview + moderated Concept testing, with the intent of gather Attitudinal and Behavioral qualitative data.

This study brought high-level learnings/information that helped not only inform the Design decisions made next, but also invalidate one of the most important decisions made by the business until that moment.

The business decision was to give an amount of "Points” per purchase as a benefit of using the card, but we learned that this proposal wasn't attractive enough, but instead of that giving Bitcoin, or other crypto as cashback was something perceived as a strong differentiator.

Those findings help the business change their decision to give crypto instead of points, which was called "Cryptoback”.
