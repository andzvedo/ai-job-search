Can you share an example of a time when you work with a less experienced designer on a challenging project, either worked together or helped coach them through it?

I remember a time during the Bitso Card MVP project when I had the opportunity to work with a less experienced Product Designer through the development of the 'Cryptoback' feature. The Design members of the Bitso Card tribe were composed of me (Staff Product Designer), another Product Designer, a Content Designer, and a UX Researcher.

The challenge began when our user research revealed a strong preference for cryptocurrency rewards over traditional points previously decided by the business stakeholders. Based on the learnings, I guided the designer through the process of translating insights into a tangible feature:

We analyzed the research findings together, and I coached the designer on how to extract actionable insights from user feedback in a way that we could explain to stakeholders why users mentioned they would prefer to receive crypto instead of points as rewards as a credit card could do it.

I encouraged the designer to brainstorm multiple approaches to implementing crypto rewards, emphasizing the importance of balancing user desires with technical feasibility, and also ensuring that Bitso remains compliant, since Bitso is a regulated cryptocurrency exchange, getting the stakeholders closer.

We collaborated on creating a clear interface as a solution for displaying Cryptoback earnings, as a way to stimulate the usage of Bitso Card from the Mexican users,

encouraging the other product designer to think critically that the solution needs to fit into the larger user experience of the Bitso Card, focusing on visual hierarchy and information architecture.

I suggested the designer present the Cryptoback solution to stakeholders, starting by emphasizing the use of research data to support the design decisions, and demonstrating how users would earn and track their Cryptoback rewards using an interactive prototype.

We discussed with the stakeholders the potential impact on user engagement and retention, as well as the technical and compliance considerations of implementing crypto rewards, and everyone agreed that Cryptoback was a better solution.

By the end of this feature development, the product designer mentioned that he gained valuable experience in translating user insights into innovative features and became more confident in presenting ideas to stakeholders.

Describe a situation where you had to mediate a disagreement within the design team. How did you handle it? What was the result?

In the design team at Blackbird.ai, there was a disagreement about the value of UX research. I strongly believed that user insights were crucial for creating truly impactful designs, but the Design Manager consistently undervalued it. He was hesitant to allocate efforts to Discovery and Research initiatives, arguing that it could slow down the pace of development.

As a result, design decisions were frequently made based on assumptions about users rather than actual user data. During grooming sessions, engineers repeatedly questioned the rationale behind our proposed solutions, causing insecurity within the team. To address this challenge, I proposed a research framework focused on efficiency and speed. By conducting targeted user interviews and usability tests, we were able to generate actionable insights without disrupting the team’s workflow.

This approach resulted in a more collaborative, evidence-driven culture, improved Design relationship with the Product and Engineering teams, and ultimately gained the full support of the Design Manager.

Tell me about a time when you had to advocate for a design vision/recommendation to stakeholders or leadership with dissenting opinions. How did you navigate the situation? What was the outcome?

At Magazine Luiza, I led the UX/UI design for the MaaS Console, a complex B2B platform. The project faced challenges due to a lack of alignment between the Product Owner and Tech Lead, which led to confusion and missed requirements. I saw an opportunity in which a clear design vision could bridge this gap.

When a critical deletion flow was forgotten, causing tension between stakeholders, I took responsibility and used this moment to push for a more comprehensive design process. I presented detailed wireframes and prototypes that demonstrated how holistically addressing the user journey would meet business goals and enhance the user experience. This approach shifted their perspective, leading them to recognize the value of integrating design more deeply into the development process.

As a result, the team’s approach evolved, with greater emphasis on user experience and strategic alignment.

Describe how you have built or contributed to the design culture in your previous roles.

In my previous role at Bitso, I led the Product Design Guild, contributing to building and enhancing the design culture.

This initiative was a side project I spearheaded. It facilitated collaborative exploration and innovative solutions within our growing team of 16 product designers. The guild served as a platform for designers to work on projects beyond their day-to-day responsibilities, encouraging creativity and cross-functional collaboration within the Design team.

To ensure a systematic approach, I organized our work into distinct phases. Phase 1 focused on Exploration and Definition, while Phase 2 concentrated on Measuring UX.

This structured methodology allowed us to tackle design challenges founded methodically and measure the impact of our solutions, promoting a culture of continuous learning and improvement.

Knowledge sharing and continuity were prioritized through comprehensive documentation of our processes, findings, and outcomes in Confluence.

This practice helped create a valuable knowledge base that team members could refer to and build upon, ensuring that insights and learning were not lost. I also established an effective communication channel with leadership, regularly reporting Guild outcomes, and keeping the leadership informed.

These Guild initiatives collectively strengthened the design culture at Bitso, creating a model that fostered innovation, collaboration, and continuous improvement. By cultivating an environment that encouraged growth and creativity, elevating the role of design within the company.
