Describe a situation where you had to mediate a disagreement within the design team. How did you handle it? What was the result?

Answer

✅ Final

In the design team at Blackbird.ai, there was a disagreement about the value of UX research. I strongly believed that user insights were crucial for creating truly impactful designs, but the Design Manager consistently undervalued it. He was hesitant to allocate efforts to Discovery and Research initiatives, arguing that it could slow down the pace of development.

As a result, design decisions were frequently made based on assumptions about users rather than actual user data. During grooming sessions, engineers repeatedly questioned the rationale behind our proposed solutions, causing insecurity within the team. To address this challenge, I proposed a research framework focused on efficiency and speed. By conducting targeted user interviews and usability tests, we were able to generate actionable insights without disrupting the team’s workflow.

This approach resulted in a more collaborative, evidence-driven culture, improved Design relationship with the Product and Engineering teams, and ultimately gained the full support of the Design Manager.

Context:

“When I joined Blackbird.ai as a Product Designer, I quickly noticed tension between the Product and Engineering teams. This tension stemmed from previous projects where complex features were developed but rarely used by users, leading to frustration and insecurity among the engineers. During grooming sessions, engineers began challenging the Product team on the rationale behind proposed solutions, which they felt were being driven by strong opinions rather than evidence-based decisions.

What I Learned:

As I partnered with the Product Design Manager on various initiatives, it became clear that the Product and Design teams were working in silos, often making decisions without involving Engineering. Furthermore, there was a significant lack of user research to validate these decisions, with strong opinions and assumptions often taking precedence over user needs. The Design Manager was initially resistant to incorporating research, fearing it would slow us down, especially given the fast-paced startup environment.

What I Did:

Recognizing the need for a more collaborative and evidence-driven approach, I proposed several initiatives to bridge the gap between teams:

• I reached out to the Sales and Customer Success teams to gather insights on user pain points and what attracted prospects during demos.

• I initiated quick internal usability testing sessions with specialists to validate design proposals before development, ensuring that we built features users actually needed.

• I advocated for designing with measurable hypotheses, where assumptions were transformed into testable propositions. This approach allowed us to challenge our solutions and better understand what would truly benefit our users.

Result:

These efforts significantly improved the relationship between the Product and Engineering teams. By involving Engineering early in the process and basing our design decisions on real user insights, we reduced guesswork and fostered a more collaborative, evidence-driven culture. Ultimately, this not only enhanced the team’s cohesion but also led to the development of more effective and user-centered solutions.”
