# Interview Preparation Guide

## STAR Format

Structure answers as: **Situation** (context), **Task** (your responsibility), **Action** (what you did), **Result** (outcome).

Keep answers to 1-2 minutes. Be specific. End with what you learned or would do differently.

## Ready-Made STAR Examples

### 1. Bitso Card Cryptoback (challenging assumptions with research)
**S:** Bitso wanted an MVP debit card so Mexican users could spend crypto. Business planned “points” as the core reward.
**T:** As Staff Product Designer on a new Card tribe (with PM, UX Researcher, Content Designer), run discovery to guide what to build for the MVP from the user perspective.
**A:** Mixed methods: in-depth interviews + moderated concept testing (attitudinal + behavioral). Synthesized findings that points were weak; crypto cashback was the differentiator. Brought evidence to stakeholders and aligned the team on **Cryptoback**.
**R:** Business pivoted reward strategy to Cryptoback; card launched toward crypto-to-fiat spend at scale. Lesson: challenge assumptions with collaborative, formative design.
**Use for:** "Tell me about influencing strategy", "disagreement with stakeholders", "research impact", fintech interviews

### 2. Blackbird Analyze / Network Graph (−39% tickets)
**S:** On Blackbird’s AI narrative intelligence platform, users’ mental models mismatched between Analyze and Network Graph; support load reflected confusion.
**T:** Clarify scope and mental models so insights were trustworthy and actionable.
**A:** Research + redesign: naming, grouping metrics, visible counts, preview before generating the graph; partnered with Eng/Product/CS; measured with usage data (Amplitude) and feedback.
**R:** Related support tickets down **14%** after month 1 and **39%** after month 2.
**Use for:** "impact / metrics", "complex data products", "AI UX", "working with CS"

### 3. Arquivei onboarding (+56% lead-to-trial)
**S:** SaaS B2B trial conversion was bottlenecked by onboarding that asked for commitment before showing value.
**T:** Redesign end-to-end trial onboarding as Senior Product Designer.
**A:** Reframed flows to demonstrate value earlier; iterative UX for trial users.
**R:** **+56%** lead-to-trial conversion (portfolio case).
**Use for:** "onboarding", "conversion", "B2B SaaS", "show impact"

### 4. Product Design Guild at Bitso (influence leadership)
**S:** ~16 product designers needed a space to mature the discipline; early meetings lacked goals.
**T:** Lead the Guild as a side initiative: shape culture/process without formal org authority.
**A:** Turned meetings into workshops; created safe voice for everyone; split into four groups on voted pain points; facilitated co-creation + async follow-through; iterated from peer feedback.
**R:** Visibility of internal issues to leadership; path toward shared guidelines; deeper relationships across the design org. Challenge: sustaining engagement on a time-consuming side project.
**Use for:** "leadership without authority", "culture", "facilitation", "mentoring"

### 5. Bitso B2B onboarding / compliance
**S:** Institutional customers needed automated onboarding across multi-jurisdiction KYC/KYB (Compliance BR/MX/AR/Gibraltar).
**T:** Contribute design/UX strategy for Bitso for Business onboarding with less regulatory friction.
**A:** Partnered with Compliance and cross-functional partners to translate requirements into clearer workflows for **1,900+** institutional customers.
**R:** Scalable B2B onboarding narrative used successfully in compliance/fraud-adjacent interviews (e.g. Sardine-style).
**Use for:** "regulated products", "B2B complexity", "stakeholder management"

### 6. MaaS at Luizalabs (proudest project / lessons)
**S:** Designed Console + Account for Magalu-as-a-Service marketplace of on-demand services for retailers with a full cross-functional squad for 6+ months.
**T:** End-to-end UX/UI for a complex B2B platform (permissions, APIs, payments, multi-step registration).
**A:** Shipped design for public/registered views, Material UI patterns, long forms, confirmation flows; pushed for feedback loops.
**R:** Leadership halted the initiative over business-value uncertainty. Career lesson: never work in a silo; challenge assumptions with design methods; keep continuous feedback. Honest about non-ship of business outcome.
**Use for:** "proudest project", "failure/learning", "complex systems", "handling cancellation"

### 7. Mind Tools LMS content editor
**S:** Mind Tools needed a content editor integrated with their LMS.
**T:** Lead design from scratch with UX Researcher and PM.
**A:** End-to-end editor UX for publishing/business-management content workflows.
**R:** Delivered 0→1 tooling as Senior PD; first major international remote Senior role.
**Use for:** "0→1", "content tools", "collaboration with research"

### 8. Overlapping roles / availability (meta)
**S:** Concurrent Toptal, The Social EMU founding work, and Blackbird history.
**T:** Explain prioritization and capacity to employers.
**A:** Be explicit about contractor vs full-time interest, conflicts of interest, and start date.
**R:** Prevents trust issues mid-process. Prepare before interviews.
**Use for:** "current commitments", "why leave", "start date"

## Common Tough Questions

### "Why did you leave [previous company]?"
> Blackbird: prepare a forward-looking end-of-engagement narrative (Mar 2026). SIA: company layoff — factual, brief, no bitterness. Bitso: growth/next challenge after Staff scope. Never trash employers.

### "You don't have [specific skill/experience]."
> Bridge: AI product design without ML engineering (Blackbird); compliance UX without being a lawyer (Bitso KYB); design–engineering fluency ≠ claiming full-stack IC.

### "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"
> Staff/Senior IC craft in AI/fintech/B2B platforms, optionally deeper influence leadership (guilds/player-coach), remote international collaboration. Align to the role’s ladder.

### "What's your biggest weakness?"
> Environment: thrives with clear priorities; disorganization drains focus (ADHD-aware). Mitigation: rituals, written goals, tight feedback loops. Do not overshare medically unless you choose to.

### "Why this company specifically?"
> Customize: product friction, mission, market position, team. Use Pattern C from writing style (product-obsessed), not generic Staff spray.

### "You are in Brazil — can you work with us?"
> Yes: years of remote work with USA/Mexico/UK teams; open to contractor / freelance / EOR; portfolio of distributed delivery. Ask about their hiring vehicle early.

## Questions You Should Ask Interviewers

### About the Role
- "What does a typical week look like in this role?"
- "What would success look like in the first 6 months?"
- "What's the biggest challenge the team is facing right now?"

### About the Team
- "How big is the design/product team, and how do you divide work?"
- "What does the lifecycle look like from insight to production?"
- "How do you onboard new designers?"

### About Hiring logistics (ask early for international roles)
- "Do you hire Brazil-based remote talent as employees, contractors, or via EOR?"
- "Is compensation in USD (or another hard currency) for international contractors?"

### About Tech & Growth
- "What's your design stack and research practice?"
- "How are design systems owned?"
- "Room to grow into architectural or strategic product decisions?"

### About Culture
- "How would you describe the team culture?"
- "Balance between net-new product work and maintenance?"
- "What do people who thrive here have in common?"

## Phone/Video Interview Tips
- Have STAR examples written out (use this file)
- Keep a glass of water nearby
- Smile when speaking (it changes your tone)
- Ask for clarification if a question is vague
- It's OK to take 5 seconds to think before answering
- End with: "Is there anything else you'd like to know about my background?"

## After the Application (Best Practice)

### Follow-Up Etiquette
- **Don't call to "stand out"** or to learn more about the role post-submission - this risks a negative impression
- If the employer specified a timeline, respect it and wait
- If no timeline was given and significant time has passed (2+ weeks), a brief call to ask about status is acceptable
- If you have genuinely new, relevant information to share, a short follow-up is fine

### Thank-You Notes
- When you receive any update (interview invitation, rejection, or status update), send a brief thank-you message
- Express appreciation for their time and the process
- Keep it short (2-3 sentences)

## Roleplay Guidelines
When the user asks for interview practice:
1. Ask which role/company to simulate
2. Start with easy warm-up questions ("Tell me about yourself")
3. Progress to role-specific technical questions
4. Include 1-2 behavioral questions using the competencies from the job posting
5. End with a tough question or curveball
6. After each answer, give brief feedback: what worked, what to sharpen
7. Suggest which STAR example would work best for each question
